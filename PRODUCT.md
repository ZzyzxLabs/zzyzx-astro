# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Protocol engineering teams and founders shipping value-bearing code, primarily on Sui / Move, secondarily EVM. They arrive in one of two states: **pre-launch**, holding a commit and a deadline, trying to decide whether this firm can be trusted with it; or **post-incident**, reading someone else's failure to understand their own exposure. Both are technical readers who will judge the firm by the quality of its findings, not by its adjectives. Secondary audience: other security researchers, for whom the published archive is the credential.

The job to be done: *decide whether these people can find the bug I can't see, and whether they can explain it to my team.*

## Product Purpose

Publish the studio's security work as evidence — audit reports and incident reconstructions in a single, consistent finding format — so that the archive itself is the sales argument. The site converts a reader into a scoping conversation. Success is a technical reader finishing one writeup and mailing about a commit.

## Brand Personality

**Studio-craft, editorial, made.** A design-led practice that happens to do security. The page should read as a deliberately constructed object — someone chose this — rather than as a template holding technical content. Voice is precise and unhurried, declarative without swagger, and it never reaches for a superlative where a specific fact would do. Evidence over assertion: the design's job is to make a finding legible and to make the reader believe the finding was found, not generated.

Emotional target: *confidence through visible care*. The reader should infer that a team this exact about a severity tally is exact about a rounding direction.

## Anti-references

- **Generic Web3 dark + neon.** Near-black canvas, cyan or violet accent, glow, glass panels, hairline-on-black everything. The default crypto-site look and the single most-recognized AI-generated tell. This is the primary anti-reference and the current site sits inside it.
- **Big-4 compliance deliverable.** Consultancy austerity — muted slate, safe serif, the visual language of a PDF nobody reads. Restraint without a point of view.
- **Crypto hype.** Gradient text, 3D coins, animated mesh backdrops, token-launch energy.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** A security firm that ships an inaccessible, unverified interface has argued against itself. Contrast, keyboard paths, and reduced-motion are brand claims here, not compliance chores.
2. **Evidence over assertion.** Every superlative is a liability. Show the finding, the commit, the severity rationale. Copy that announces an outcome ("closed every finding") is weaker than copy that names a mechanism ("let a redeemer round the pool down").
3. **Signal is scarce, so spend it.** Colour, weight, and emphasis are a budget. When every row is highlighted, nothing is. Severity is the one axis that earns colour.
4. **Designed, not scaffolded.** Eyebrows, numbered markers, uniform reveals and badge racks applied by reflex are the tell. Every repeated device must be able to answer "why here and not everywhere".
5. **The archive is the product.** Marketing sections support the writeups; the writeups are not filler between marketing sections. Reading quality wins over hero impact wherever they conflict.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor and is treated as a release gate: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI boundaries, visible focus on every interactive element, complete keyboard paths, and a genuine reduced-motion alternative for every animation. Severity must never be encoded by hue alone — the label and count carry it for colour-blind and monochrome readers.
